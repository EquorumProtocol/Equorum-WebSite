For investors

You are lending to a protocol, one tranche at a time.

A revenue bond pays you a fixed minimum on a fixed schedule, plus a share of revenue when the issuer routes it. What makes it different from a promise is where the money sits while you wait.

What you own

The instrument

An ERC-20 bond

Transferable, divisible, and yours to sell to anyone. Your claim travels with the token.

The payments

A minimum coupon

Set at issuance and never changed. Across the full term the minimum adds up to at least 100% of what the bond raised.

The upside

A revenue share

Anything the issuer routes above the minimum is distributed pro-rata, and counts toward the schedule.

The two endings

Every bond finishes one of two ways. Both are settled by the contract, not by a conversation.

The issuer pays through the term

You received every coupon on schedule plus any revenue share on top. The bond matures, and the issuer takes the escrow it had already earned the right to draw.

Your return: at least 100% of what you paid, plus whatever the terms priced above that.

The issuer misses a coupon

Past the grace period, anyone — including you — can trigger the default. The tap closes permanently.

Your recovery: the coupons already paid, plus your pro-rata share of the capital the issuer never drew, plus the collateral it posted. You burn your bonds to claim it.

Read this before you buy

  • Initial release. How much the issuer draws on day one. That is your unprotected exposure from the start.
  • Coupon size and period. Can this protocol cover it from a bad month, not a good one?
  • Collateral. Extra ETH the issuer locked. On default it is distributed with the undrawn capital.
  • Payment history. The registry records every raise, every payment and every default, permanently.
  • Who the issuer is. A contract cannot tell you whether a team will still be around in nine months.

Risks, plainly

Credit risk. Capital the issuer has already drawn is not protected beyond the collateral. A default can still lose you money.

Revenue risk. The share above the minimum is only as good as the protocol's revenue, and the issuer decides what flows through the router until Locked Revenue ships.

Smart contract risk. V3 has 82 tests and no external audit yet. The first audit is scheduled for [AUDIT DATE].

Liquidity risk. Bonds are transferable, but there may be no one to sell to before maturity.
