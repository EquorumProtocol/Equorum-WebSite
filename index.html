Every way you fund a protocol today costs you the protocol.

Token sale

You pay with dilution

New supply, permanent sell pressure, and a holder base that judges you by a chart instead of a product.

VC round

You pay with control

Months of term sheets, a jurisdiction to pick, a board to answer to — for capital you needed last quarter.

Treasury swap

You pay with conviction

Selling the asset you tell everyone to hold, at the exact moment you least want to sell it.

A revenue bond costs you none of the three. You sell a slice of what you earn, for a fixed term, and when the term ends the slice comes back to you.

How it works

The capital arrives through a tap, not a faucet.

01

You set the terms

Amount, term, minimum coupon, how much you draw on day one. Once the bond is created, nobody can change any of it — not you, not us.

02

Investors fund the escrow

They buy ERC-20 bonds at a fixed price. The ETH stays in the contract, not in your wallet and never in ours.

03

You draw as you pay

Each tranche unlocks only after the coupons due so far are paid. Pay early and you keep the schedule; stop paying and the tap stops with you.

04

The term ends, or it doesn't

Pay through the term and the bond closes. Miss a coupon past the grace period and anyone can end it: holders take back the undrawn capital and your collateral.

What the contract enforces

Undrawn capital belongs to holders. On default it can only move to them, plus whatever collateral you posted.

You can't outrun your payment record. The tap reads it directly; there is no manual override anywhere in the system.

Minimum payments cover the principal. A bond that runs its full term returns at least 100% of what was raised.

No admin keys over live bonds. The multisig can set the protocol fee for new bonds and pause new ones. That is all it can do.

What it doesn't

The three things a marketing page usually hides.

Yield isn't guaranteed. A bond pays what the issuer pays. The escrow protects the part not yet drawn, nothing more.

Drawn capital carries credit risk. Beyond posted collateral, an issuer that stops paying leaves holders with what they already received.

Worked example

100 ETH raised. 120 ETH returned over a year.

You draw 25 ETH on day one and the rest over the first quarter, one tranche per coupon paid. From month four you are paying down. If you stop at month two, holders recover the 50 ETH still in escrow plus your collateral — and keep the 20 ETH you already paid.

Protocol fee: 2% of the capital you actually draw. Nothing on what you don't.

For investors

Questions issuers actually ask

Do I need a token?

No. The bond is its own ERC-20. If you have a token, nothing about it changes — no supply, no governance, no unlock.

What if my revenue drops?

You keep paying the minimum coupon from wherever you like, or you default. Set a coupon you can cover on a bad quarter, not a good one.

What does it cost?

2% of the capital you draw, capped at 3% forever. No listing fee, no fee on a sale that doesn't fill, no fee on coupons.
